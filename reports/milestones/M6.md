# M6 — controlled study and final research report

## Acceptance evidence

The first scoped M0–M6 study is complete: a verified baseline, controlled A/B
comparisons, actual serialized payloads, repeated CPU measurements, configurable
queue/capacity models, a verified/synthesized RTL subset, four headline figures,
failure cases, reproduction tooling and a paper-style report. This is not a
claim of publication novelty, universal algorithm safety or physical FPGA speed.

Main dataset: 36 points (X/Z, d=3/5/7, rounds=d/2d, p=.003/.005/.01), 30,000 shots
each. Both policies reuse identical samples with baseline. Additional controls:
four 10,000-shot noiseless/high-noise X/Z points. Independent Sinter replication:
30,000 shots on one point. RTL replay: 30,000 saved shots each for X d=3/5/7 and
Z d=3 at p=.005, rounds=d, with actual output frames/residuals scored through MWPM.

Results and interpretation are in [the final report](../final_report.md).
Unrestricted preprocessing is slower on CPU and has pointwise evidence of
accuracy degradation. Serialized communication savings depend strongly on format.
The strict policy has no observed prediction disagreement but smaller savings.
RTL exactly implements the tested heuristic, including its unfavorable outcomes.

## Issues encountered and resolved

The previous temporary Linux environment no longer existed. Verification stopped
before simulation; a persistent native environment resolved this. Queue export
initially rejected a NumPy integer; explicit native integer conversion and a JSON
serialization regression check fixed it. Timings were repeated sequentially
after generation/builds to remove self-imposed workload overlap. CPU context:
Intel i7-1260P, 16 visible logical CPUs, shared/unpinned Windows environment.

The study answers a bounded research question for this candidate and grid. It
does not establish a threshold, equivalence margin, physical FPGA frequency,
power, performance, or arbitrary streaming correction. Independent Sinter
replication uses internally managed randomness. Cross-platform bitwise sampling
identity is not assumed. Queue clock assumptions remain hypothetical; finite
capacity infeasibility is reported without dropping work.
